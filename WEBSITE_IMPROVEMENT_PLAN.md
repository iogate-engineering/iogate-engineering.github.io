# iogate Website Improvement Plan

This document captures the website review and provides a checklist for improving the site one item at a time.

## 1. Add concrete credibility

The website explains what iogate can do, but prospective clients need more evidence that iogate is a safe and credible choice.

Potential additions:

- A concise founder or team profile.
- Relevant years of professional experience.
- The kinds of organisations, systems, and environments worked with.
- Relevant certifications and technology credentials.
- Named client outcomes where permission exists.
- Anonymised examples where client names or sensitive details cannot be shared.
- Specific, defensible results, such as improvements to deployment time, reliability, security, cloud cost, or audit readiness.
- Links to professional profiles such as LinkedIn.

All claims must be accurate and supportable. Confidential client information should not be published.

## 2. Make the target customer more specific — Complete

### Agreed positioning

- Customers may be based anywhere; do not constrain the message to the UK.
- Primary buyer: founders.
- Best fit: startups, growing teams, and smaller technology organisations that need senior cloud expertise without a large internal platform team.
- AWS is the primary specialism; Azure remains a supported capability.
- Kubernetes is a specialist strength and should be presented as deep expertise rather than grouped as another cloud provider.
- Do not position specifically around public-sector customers.
- Use build, launch, and scale as the umbrella journey rather than selecting one lead service prematurely.
- Engagements are led by the two directors, with additional trusted capacity available when needed.

- [x] Made deep Kubernetes expertise explicit in the hero and trust messaging.

## 3. Turn services into defined offers

Package capabilities into bounded, easier-to-buy engagements, potentially including:

- Cloud Foundation Assessment
- AWS/Azure Landing Zone Sprint
- Platform Engineering Roadmap
- FinOps Readiness Review
- Production-Ready Proof of Concept

For each offer, explain the ideal customer, problem addressed, duration, deliverables, engagement process, and expected outcome.

## Ongoing copy improvement

- Replace broad consultancy phrases with concrete descriptions where possible.
- Describe pains buyers already recognise: fragile deployments, inconsistent environments, uncontrolled cloud costs, audit pressure, manual work, and systems understood by only one engineer.
- Prefer specific, supportable outcomes over general claims.

## 8. Refine the visual design

The current design is professional, credible, and appropriate for a secure cloud consultancy. Future work should refine the existing visual system rather than replace it.

- Reduce content density in text-heavy tiles, particularly the director profiles.
- Make profile and credibility information easier to scan.
- Align heading areas and paragraph starting positions across the experience cards.
- Introduce modest visual variation between sections so the page does not rely on the same rounded-panel treatment everywhere.
- Consider replacing the oversized logo inside the hero card with something more informative, such as delivery outcomes, key metrics, or a concise architecture visual.
- Review the complete updated site at mobile and tablet widths, where longer copy may have the greatest effect.
